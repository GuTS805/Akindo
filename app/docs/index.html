How mbLP works

The mechanics, the safety design, the contracts and the limits, in plain language.

Overview

mbLP is a vault on Mezo. You deposit BTC and receive mbLP shares. The vault keeps about 75% of the BTC as collateral in one MUSD trove, borrows MUSD against it, pairs that MUSD with the other 25% of the BTC in the volatile BTC/MUSD pool on Mezo Pools, and stakes the LP tokens in the pool's gauge. Gauge rewards are credited to shareholders.

Your exposure stays about 1x BTC: the MUSD in the pool roughly offsets the MUSD debt. The vault has no owner and no admin key; its parameters are fixed at deployment and rebalancing and harvesting are open to anyone.

The position

At its target the vault holds, for a debt of D MUSD:

  • Collateral: BTC worth 3·D in the trove (300% collateral ratio).
  • LP: D MUSD plus BTC worth D in the pool, staked in the gauge.
  • Net value: 3D + 2D − D = 4D, so the debt target is NAV / (targetCR + 1).

Shares are priced from Mezo's oracle, never from the pool. LP is valued at 2·√(k·price), the value of the pool's reserves if they were traded to the oracle price, which trading the pool cannot inflate.

Rebalancing

When BTC's price moves, the collateral ratio drifts. Anyone can call rebalance() once it leaves the 250–350% band or once idle BTC exceeds 1% of value. The vault then moves debt back to NAV / (targetCR + 1) without swapping:

  • Ratio too high: borrow more MUSD, take matching BTC from collateral and add both to the pool.
  • Ratio too low: remove LP, repay debt with its MUSD and put its BTC back as collateral.

Adding liquidity and rebalancing first check that the pool price is within the oracle band (±6% on testnet, ±2% planned for mainnet), so a manipulated pool makes them wait instead of trading at a bad price. Exits skip that check so you can always leave; a test suite shows that pushing the pool to profit from someone's exit costs the attacker more than it gains. A GitHub Action calls rebalance() and harvest() on a schedule (every 30 minutes requested; GitHub runs it every few hours); anyone else can too.

Exits

A withdrawal takes your share of every position: idle assets, LP, debt and collateral. If BTC has fallen, your LP slice holds less MUSD than your debt slice. That gap is settled inside the vault: you leave the gap debt in the trove plus collateral worth the gap at the oracle price + 0.5%. The remaining holders come out slightly ahead and the thin pool is not touched. If that would push the trove under 250%, the gap is bought on the DEX instead, with a minimum output taken from the oracle.

If your exit would leave less than MUSD's 1,800 minimum debt, the vault unwinds: it removes all LP, closes the trove and pays you your share. The other holders keep theirs as idle BTC and MUSD until deposits make the vault large enough to reopen a trove. A large holder is never stuck behind small ones.

After a MUSD liquidation the vault holds the old LP and no trove, and keeps earning as plain LP. After a full redemption, the surplus collateral is claimed automatically before anyone is priced.

Rewards and fees

The gauge streams rewards (MEZO on mainnet) to staked LP. Staked LPs do not earn swap fees; those go to voters. harvest() pulls rewards into the vault, sends 10% to the fee recipient and credits the rest per share. Claims stay exact across share transfers: the vault harvests before any transfer.

MUSD interest (1% a year on the vault's trove) accrues on the debt and is paid from the position when debt is repaid.

The fee flywheel

Mezo Earn splits MEZO emissions between gauges by veBTC votes, and protocols attract those votes by posting incentives on a gauge. With an IncentiveRouter as the vault's fee recipient, anyone can push part of the collected MEZO onto the BTC/MUSD gauge's incentive contract, so the vault's own fees buy votes for its own pool: more deposits, more fees, more votes, more emissions, higher yield. On a Mezo mainnet fork, one day of fees from a 0.2 BTC deposit posted 184 MEZO on the live gauge's incentive contract.

Two testnet vaults

Mezo's official testnet BTC/MUSD gauge has no emissions, and only Mezo's Voter can fund it, so a vault staked there never has anything to harvest. The same vault contract is therefore deployed twice. The MEZO rewards vault stakes in a small RewardGauge with the same interface, which anyone can fund with testnet MEZO; there, rewards accrue every second and harvest and claim pay out for real. The Official gauge vault stakes in Mezo's gauge, as the vault would on mainnet. Switch between them at the top of the Vault, Portfolio and Analytics pages.

Safety and testing

  • 32 unit, fuzz and recovery tests, covering liquidation, redemption, transfers, donations and slippage, plus 9 for the reward gauge, 6 for the incentive router and 18 edge cases (config guards, stale oracle, slippage, the last exit after a fall). 97% line and 100% function coverage.
  • An invariant suite of random action sequences (65,536 actions across 1,024 runs, including pool manipulation, liquidations and redemptions), checking that shares and rewards add up, the trove keeps MUSD's rules and every holder can exit.
  • A mainnet fork test runs the same cycle against mainnet MUSD, the BTC/MUSD pool and the live MEZO gauge: 0.2 BTC earned 3,309 MEZO in a day and returned 0.19995 BTC.
  • CI runs every test and both fork tests on each push. Slither's 32 results are each reviewed in SECURITY.md.
  • A fork test of a full deposit and withdraw cycle against the deployed testnet MUSD and Mezo Pools contracts (0.9997 BTC back per BTC).
  • The tests found five real bugs before deployment; each fix is its own commit in the repository.
  • Source verified on the Mezo explorer. Reentrancy guards, dead shares against first-depositor inflation, oracle-based minimum outputs on every swap.
  • Not audited.

Risks and limits

  • Impermanent loss on the ~25% of value in the pool when BTC's price moves.
  • MEZO rewards have little on-chain liquidity today. The vault pays them out and never sells them.
  • Stale oracle: MUSD reads the oracle on every trove change, so deposits, rebalances and exits wait while it is stale.
  • MUSD recovery mode (system collateral ratio under 150%): MUSD blocks closing troves and restricts collateral withdrawals, so exits that need either wait.
  • The last exit buys the LP's MUSD gap on the DEX and waits rather than sell BTC below the oracle band, which can bind on a thin mainnet pool.
  • Yield dilution: the APR falls as more liquidity joins the gauge.
  • Unaudited hackathon code, deployed on testnet.

FAQ

Is this leverage?

No. The MUSD in the pool roughly matches the MUSD debt, so exposure stays about 1x BTC. It is BTC plus yield.

Can the vault be liquidated?

The trove targets 300% and is rebalanced below 250%, far from MUSD's 110% liquidation level. If it were liquidated anyway, the vault keeps the LP and continues as plain LP.

Why does a withdrawal sometimes return MUSD?

When BTC has risen, your LP slice holds more MUSD than your debt slice; the extra MUSD is yours.

Who runs it?

Nobody needs to. Rebalance and harvest are permissionless; a public keeper runs on a schedule, every few hours in practice.

Can I use a Bitcoin wallet?

Yes. Choose Unisat, OKX or Xverse under "Bitcoin wallets · Mezo Passport" when connecting. Mezo Passport gives your Bitcoin wallet a Mezo smart account: your wallet signs, the smart account deposits and withdraws, and Mezo's relayer submits the transaction. The smart account's address is shown in the header; send it testnet BTC from the faucet first.

What is next?

The concentrated BTC/MUSD pool (which gets the larger gauge share), and optional veMEZO compounding, then mainnet.
